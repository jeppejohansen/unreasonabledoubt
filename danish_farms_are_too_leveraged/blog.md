
# Some ideas for dealing with Danish farms

*Danish Farms are leveraged*: Already danish farms have almost 70% of leverage (real credit) -- maybe even more. And they are allowed to have 70%. Also, they have a total of 250 billion danish kr in outstanding debt. More than 10% of danish GDP. This makes Danish farm extremely sensitive to regulation, and the individuals holding real credit debt, are exposed. Additionally, it's probably not that political feasible to see mass farm closures.

*Danish farms land-value have sharply increased in later years* (heard in podcast, find evidence).

*The basic model of the value of farm (as measured by land value) is: Land is almost inelastic. The price shows the future discounted cash flows, and what leverage level can sustain these. I.e. Subsidization to farms will just result in more expensive farm land. Profits are still competed away (at least after transfer of farm land). I.e. subsidies is a transfer to current land holders (i.e. regressive).

*Dealing with leveraged farms*: The best way to deal with leveraged farms (as well as implementing a green transition) is, to slowly reduce the allowed debt farms can have. Read the papers to model it.

*Also do cap-and-trade for polution*: It's infeasible to very heavily reduce pesticides etc. Since it will hit the revenue (lower yields), and cost side (higher costs for alternative sources of crop treatments), very leveraged farms will maybe not be going concerns. I.e. these needs to slowly integrated. The cap and trade system for CO2 in Europe shows this is feasible. Concretely, what could be done is the following: By each year reduce the maximal allowed outstanding debt by 3% percent. 70%, next 67%, so on so forth until sustainable level it achieved. We can then consider the (nominal) land value price corridor. Since debt problems are in nominal terms (i.e. whether you can pay the debt), the nominal price coridor, should make almost flat price increase in land value. That means a hektor today of 100.000 dkk, should stay at that level until both the aggregate leverage target (say 30 %) and green transition target (say 90% reduction to baseline), have been achieved. In that sense quotas for the green transition (and the allowed debt level) changes can be nudged up and down, depending on how the price level evolves.
